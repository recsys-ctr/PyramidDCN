# Slice topology, permutation equivalence, and an exact separation

Structural analysis of fixed input connections in three-cross PyramidDCN. This document provides proofs and scope limits; it does not claim a verified prior-art priority or universal prediction advantage.

## 1. Model and scope

Let the fixed embedded input be \(y\in\mathbb R^D\), and let \(x=Py\in\mathbb R^W\) be a projected input with an arbitrary learned dense matrix \(P\). A three-layer cross network has

\[
h_i=R_i x\odot(A_i x_{i-1}+b_i)+R_i x,
\qquad x_i=\operatorname{LN}_i(h_i),\qquad x_0=x,
\]

followed by the affine logit \(w^\top x_3+b_0\). Here \(R_i\) is a fixed coordinate-gather matrix. The permutation result below includes layer normalization. The polynomial results replace LN by the identity, and concern the homogeneous degree-four part of the logit. Arbitrary cross biases and a head bias do not change that part.

Set \(W=4k\), use widths \([4k,2k,k]\), and let \(R_1=I_W\). Write \(U\) for the middle-layer coordinates and \(V\) for the last-layer coordinates, of sizes \(2k\) and \(k\). With \(M=A_1\), \(A=A_2\), and \(B=\operatorname{diag}(w)A_3\), the quartic is

\[
F_4(x)=v^\top B\bigl[u\odot A(x\odot Mx)\bigr],
\qquad u=R_2x,\quad v=R_3x.
\tag{1}
\]

Setting every \(b_i=-\mathbf 1\) cancels the input addends and realizes a pure quartic of this form exactly. Dense cross/head cost is \(26k^2+k\) MACs; the common projected-input cost adds \(WD\). All constructions below keep these same matrix shapes and this same cost convention.

## 2. Global coordinate permutation is an exact equivalence

Suppose two selector patterns obey

\[
R_i'Q=T_iR_i \quad (i=1,2,3),
\tag{2}
\]

where \(Q\) permutes the projected input coordinates and \(T_i\) permutes the corresponding layer's channels. Put \(T_0=Q\), and transform parameters as

\[
P'=QP,\qquad A_i'=T_iA_iT_{i-1}^{\top},\qquad
b_i'=T_ib_i,\qquad w'=T_3w.
\tag{3}
\]

Permute each LN scale and offset by \(T_i\) as well. Equation (2), the identity \((Ta)\odot(Tb)=T(a\odot b)\), and permutation invariance of the mean/variance used by LN imply inductively that \(x_i'=T_ix_i\). The logits are identical. The transformation is invertible, preserves each matrix rank, and preserves dense and two-factor matrix-product costs.

For subsets without repeated indices and with \(S_1\) equal to all coordinates, the complete incidence invariant is

\[
t=|U\cap V|.
\]

Indeed, the four coordinate classes have sizes \(t,2k-t,k-t,k+t\), corresponding to membership in both, middle only, last only, and neither. Equal class sizes give a global permutation, and the within-layer output ordering can be absorbed into the \(T_i\). With repeated gathers, the full incidence/multiplicity pattern is required instead.

**Consequence.** With a free dense learned projection, numerical spacing alone has no representational privilege: evenly spaced and contiguous selectors with the same incidence pattern define the same hypothesis class, including with LN. The potentially meaningful architectural choice is overlap and reuse across layers. A frozen or constrained projection can invalidate this equivalence. For a full-row-rank projection, \(t\) is also the dimension of the intersection of the two gate-row spaces. A rank-deficient projection can create extra coincidences; it cannot invalidate the necessary constraints below.

## 3. Nested slices impose a closed singular-subspace constraint

Assume \(V\subset U\). Each term in (1) contains one middle gate from \(U\) and one final gate from \(V\subset U\). Consequently

\[
F_4\in I(U)^2,
\]

where \(I(U)\) is the ideal generated by the middle gate forms. On the linear space \(L=\ker(R_2P)\), both \(F_4\) and all of its first partial derivatives vanish. Thus the real singular locus contains a linear subspace of dimension at least \(D-2k\). No invertibility of \(P\) is needed.

This remains a constraint under arbitrary limits of nested-network quartic coefficients. To see this, define

\[
\mathcal C_{D,k}=\{F\text{ homogeneous quartic}:\ \exists L\in
\operatorname{Gr}(D-2k,D),\ \nabla F|_L=0\}.
\]

The incidence condition is closed and the real Grassmannian is compact, so \(\mathcal C_{D,k}\) is closed in coefficient space. One may equivalently take a convergent subsequence of the witness subspaces. Every nested-network quartic and every coefficient limit of such quartics belongs to \(\mathcal C_{D,k}\).

## 4. A disjoint-slice family outside the entire nested closure

Assume \(U\cap V=\varnothing\), leaving \(k\) private coordinates \(T\). Reorder coordinates using Section 2, and write each block as \((a_i,b_i,v_i,t_i)\), with \(a_i,b_i\in U\), \(v_i\in V\), and \(t_i\in T\). Consider

\[
F_{\mathrm{dis}}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}
v_i\left[a_i(a_i^2+t_i^2+v_i^2)+b_i^3\right].
\tag{4}
\]

It is exactly realizable with the stated widths:

* First layer: all \(4k\) coordinate squares, by \(M=I_W\).
* Middle layer: \(a_i(a_i^2+t_i^2+v_i^2)\) and \(b_i^3\).
* Last layer: multiply the sum of those two states by \(v_i\), and sum the last states in the head.

Every first-, middle-, and last-layer channel contributes to the final polynomial. There is no inactive-channel padding.

For a single block, write \(F=v[a(a^2+t^2+v^2)+b^3]\). The derivative

\[
\partial_aF=v(3a^2+t^2+v^2)
\]

cannot vanish at a real point with \(v\ne0\). At \(v=0\), all derivatives except \(\partial_vF\) vanish, and \(\partial_vF=a^3+b^3+at^2\). Hence

\[
\operatorname{Sing}_{\mathbb R}(F)
=\{v=0,\ a^3+b^3+at^2=0\}.
\tag{5}
\]

The ternary cubic \(G=a^3+b^3+at^2\) contains no real two-dimensional linear subspace. Such a subspace would be a plane and its defining linear form would divide \(G\). If the plane can be solved as \(b=\alpha a+\beta t\), the coefficient of \(t^3\) forces \(\beta=0\), after which the coefficient of \(at^2\) remains one. Otherwise its equation involves only \(a,t\), and restricting \(G\) to the plane leaves the nonzero coefficient of \(b^3\). Thus no plane is contained in (5).

The blocks use disjoint variables, so their singular loci form a Cartesian product. A linear subspace in this product projects into a linear subspace of each block's singular locus, of dimension at most one. The combined projections are injective, so its dimension is at most \(k\). Therefore \(F_{\mathrm{dis}}\) has no \(2k\)-dimensional singular linear subspace in \(\mathbb R^{4k}\).

For \(D\ge W\), take \(P\) to extract these \(W\) input coordinates and ignore the others. The singular locus acquires \(D-W\) free directions; its largest possible singular linear subspace has dimension at most

\[
k+(D-W)=D-3k < D-2k.
\]

It follows that

\[
F_{\mathrm{dis}}\notin\mathcal C_{D,k}.
\tag{6}
\]

**Exact conclusion.** For every \(k\ge1\) and \(D\ge4k\), the disjoint-slice model of widths \([4k,2k,k]\) realizes a quartic outside the coefficient closure of every nested-slice model of the same dimensions and cost, even when the competing model learns an unrelated dense input projection and arbitrary biases. This is a structural input-connection separation, not an assumed small reconstruction error or a parameter-count comparison.

### Finite binary-prediction consequence

Choose any finite set of raw embedded inputs that determines every degree-at-most-four polynomial uniquely. One explicit choice is

\[
\mathcal G_D=\{\alpha/4:\ \alpha\in\mathbb N_0^D,\ |\alpha|\le4\},
\]

where \(\mathbb N_0\) denotes the nonnegative integers and \(|\alpha|=\sum_j\alpha_j\). The falling-factorial basis \(\prod_j\binom{4y_j}{\beta_j}\), ordered by total degree, gives a triangular evaluation matrix with diagonal one, proving injectivity. Put uniform mass on this set and assign label probability \(\sigma(F_{\mathrm{dis}}(y))\). The disjoint network attains Bayes LogLoss. Nested networks have a strictly positive infimum excess LogLoss: otherwise strict propriety at each of the finitely many support points would force logits to converge to the target at every point; polynomial interpolation would force coefficient convergence, contradicting (6). This supplies an existential task-level risk gap, with no quantitative lower bound and no claim about the real CTR benchmarks. Inputs are fixed embedded vectors here; this argument does not authorize freely changing their embedding lookup table.

## 5. The reverse exact inclusion also fails

A same-shape nested network can realize

\[
F_{\mathrm{nest}}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^k
v_i^2(u_i^2+v_i^2+s_i^2+t_i^2),
\tag{7}
\]

using nested middle gates \((u_i,v_i)\) and last gate \(v_i\). Its first states are \((u_iv_i,v_i^2,s_i^2,t_i^2)\); its middle states are \((u_i^2v_i,\ v_i(v_i^2+s_i^2+t_i^2))\); the last layer multiplies their sum by \(v_i\). Again every channel contributes.

The real zero set of (7) is exactly the linear plane \(V=0\), of codimension \(k\). It also depends essentially on all \(W\) variables: in each block, a zero directional derivative of \(v^2(u^2+v^2+s^2+t^2)\) has a \(vu^2\) coefficient forcing the \(v\)-direction to be zero, followed by \(uv^2,sv^2,tv^2\) coefficients forcing all other directions to be zero. Separate blocks cannot cancel these distinct monomials.

Suppose a disjoint-slice model with its own learned \(P'\) had this quartic. Essentiality forces \(P'\) to have full row rank \(W\): its kernel must be contained in the target's translation-invariance space, which has dimension \(D-W\). The middle and last gate-row spaces are therefore disjoint. Yet every disjoint-model quartic vanishes on both \(\ker V'\) and \(\ker U'\). Since the target zero set is precisely \(\ker V\), and \(\ker V'\) and \(\ker V\) have equal dimension, they must be equal. Then

\[
\ker U'\subset\ker V'\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{row}(V')\subset\operatorname{row}(U'),
\]

contradicting disjoint gate-row spaces. Thus (7) is not represented exactly by any disjoint-slice model of the same dimensions and cost.

**Closure caution.** This converse argument uses full row rank for an exact representation. Independent gate-row spaces can approach coincident spaces as parameters diverge or \(P'\) becomes ill-conditioned. It has not been shown that (7) lies outside the *closure* of disjoint models. Do not infer a reverse positive risk gap.

## 6. What this establishes, and what it does not

At exactly the default halving widths and the same matrix-product budget, overlap topology can change attainable multiplicative interactions in a way that a free learned input projection cannot always remove. Disjoint versus nested slices define incomparable exact polynomial families. Moreover, disjoint slices permit an all-channel-active target that nested slices cannot even approximate arbitrarily in coefficients, yielding a finite-task LogLoss separation.

This is not a theorem that disjoint slices always improve accuracy, that evenly spaced indices are intrinsically superior, or that narrowing beats every constant-width model. It does not show class containment, a quantitative empirical effect size, or the corresponding singular-locus constraint after LN. Permutation equivalence does include LN; the separation does not. Existing benchmark controls should continue to determine whether this additional structural freedom is useful for a particular dataset.

The potentially useful interpretation is therefore narrower and more concrete than generic compression: **non-nested parameter-free input connections avoid forcing both successive multiplicative gates into the same learned gate subspace.** That freedom has a provable fixed-cost representational consequence even when all input-projection coefficients are trainable.
